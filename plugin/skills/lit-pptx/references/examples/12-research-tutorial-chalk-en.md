# The four titration steps
---

---
layout: process

## Titration steps: prepare, titrate, compare, report

- **1. Prepare** a standardised NaOH solution and a vinegar diluted from 10.00 mL to 100.0 mL
  (1) Who and how long: the analyst, about 35 minutes with a balance and a volumetric flask
  (2) Check: the base is standardised against a weighed primary standard, to four figures
- **2. Titrate** five 25.00 mL aliquots, one rough run first, then single drops at the end
  (1) Who and how long: the analyst at the burette, about 40 minutes for six runs
  (2) Check: every burette reading is taken at eye level, to 0.01 mL
- **3. Compare** the titres and keep only the concordant ones
  (1) Who and how long: the analyst in the spreadsheet, about 10 minutes
  (2) Check: titres agree within 0.10 mL, and no trend runs through the order
- **4. Report** the concentration with its 95% interval from the titres
  (1) Who and how long: the analyst, then a second person checks the sheet
  (2) Check: the colour change arrives with the last drop

## Titration equation and symbols

c = c(NaOH) × V(NaOH) ÷ V(aliquot) × D

- **Use** the equation converts a titre into the concentration in the original vinegar
- **c(NaOH)** concentration of the standardised base, 0.1012 mol/L
- **V(NaOH)** titre, the volume of base from the burette, in mL
- **V(aliquot)** volume of diluted vinegar in the flask, 25.00 mL
- **D** dilution factor, 100.0 ÷ 10.00 = 10
- **Endpoint** the drop that turns the indicator pale pink and holds for 30 seconds
- **Concordant** titres that agree within 0.10 mL of each other
- **Interval** the range the titres allow for c, here ± 0.001 mol/L at 95%
- **Example** 0.1012 × 21.35 ÷ 25.00 × 10 = 0.864 mol/L
- **Mass** 0.864 mol/L × 60.05 g/mol = 51.9 g/L, or 5.19% w/v
- **Stoichiometry** one mole of NaOH neutralises one mole of acetic acid, so no ratio enters the equation
- **Indicator** phenolphthalein, one to two drops, which adds less than 0.05 mL to the titre
- **Parallax** a reading error that appears when the eye sits above or below the meniscus
- **One drop** about 0.05 mL, which is 0.23% of a 21.35 mL titre

## Four common titration mistakes

- **Overshooting the endpoint**
  (1) One extra drop is 0.05 mL, and a portion of 1 mL adds twenty times that
  (2) Run a rough titration first, then go dropwise over the last 2 mL
- **Skipping the rinse of the burette**
  (1) Water left in the tip dilutes the base and pushes every titre up
  (2) Rinse twice with a few mL of the NaOH solution before filling
- **Reading from above or below the meniscus**
  (1) Parallax moves a reading by 0.05 mL or more, as much as a drop
  (2) Place the eye level with the bottom of the meniscus every time
- **Skipping the concordance check**
  (1) A mean of titres that differ by 0.4 mL hides a poor run; the practice run looked fine in its mean
  (2) Repeat until three titres agree within 0.10 mL

## Takeaways and practice for this week

- Titrate a rough run first, and add the last 2 mL one drop at a time
- Standardise the base; a drifting base moves every result by the same amount
- Read the spread before the mean: concordant titres agree within 0.10 mL
- Report the concentration with the interval the titres give you, and the base strength
