# Structuring techniques

## Morphological box

For answers that have separable dimensions.

1. List four to six dimensions every answer must settle: who acts, when, where it runs, what triggers it, how it is delivered, how it is paid for.
2. List three to five options per dimension, odd ones included.
3. Lay out the grid and pick combinations: three at random, two the obvious way, two that deliberately contrast. Each combination is an idea tagged `[morph:A2-B1-C3]`.
4. Mark combinations that cannot work and keep them visible with the reason.

| Dimension | Option 1 | Option 2 | Option 3 | Option 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Trigger | manual | schedule | event | prediction |
| Where it runs | local | server | edge | user device |

## Persona lenses

Pick three to five personas that differ in goal, skill and context: a novice, an expert, a skeptic, someone with accessibility needs, someone in a hurry, the operator who supports the result. For each persona write two ideas from their point of view and one thing they would refuse. Tag `[persona:novice]`.

## Six hats

One pass per viewpoint, one to three ideas each.

- White: what data would open new options?
- Red: what would feel good or bad to use?
- Black: what would a careful critic fear? The idea form is a way to defuse the fear.
- Yellow: where is the largest upside?
- Green: wild alternatives.
- Blue: how to organize the work.

Black-hat output stays idea-shaped, as a mitigation or an alternative. A verdict waits for convergence.

## Role storming

Name three roles with a stake in the problem, such as a customer, a maintainer and a competitor. Brainstorm in the first person of each: "If I were the competitor, I would…". Roles that conflict are the useful ones. Tag `[role:competitor]`.
