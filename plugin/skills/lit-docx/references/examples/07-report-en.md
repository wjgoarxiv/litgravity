# Summary

The quarter met two of its three service targets. Trip numbers and station availability both improved, helped by the night rebalancing route introduced in July that restocks the ten busiest stations before the morning commute. Repair turnaround did not meet its target: the median e-bike spent 3.1 days in the workshop in September against a target of 2 days, because the team worked two mechanics short for most of the month.

::: keyfigures
- **412,000** trips in the quarter (sample)
  - July-September 2026, trip log; Q2 was 378,000
- **94%** station availability (sample)
  - Share of station-hours with a bike and a free dock; target 92 %
- **3.1 days** median repair turnaround in September (sample)
  - Workshop system, e-bikes only; target 2.0 days
:::

For the fourth quarter the team asks for two mechanic posts to be advertised now rather than in January, and for an order of twelve battery swap cabinets to be placed before the winter works freeze. Together these should bring repair turnaround back under target before the spring season, which opens in March.

# Trip volumes

## Trips by user type

Annual members remain the largest group but grew slowly; almost all of the quarter's increase came from day passes. Pay-per-ride trips fell for the third quarter in a row as more casual riders buy the day pass, which is cheaper from the second trip.

Table 1. Trips by user type (sample data)

| User type | Q2 2026 | Q3 2026 | Change (%) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Annual members | 241,000 | 252,000 | 4.6 |
| Day passes | 87,000 | 118,000 | 35.6 |
| Pay per ride | 50,000 | 42,000 | -16.0 |
| Total | 378,000 | 412,000 | 9.0 |

Source: trip log and ticket sales, April-September 2026 (sample)

## Trips by zone

Riverside and the university district made up 41 % of trips, the old town 29 % and the outer suburbs 18 %. The remaining 12 % started in the three new stations opened in August. Suburban stations are the weakest on availability, though they are also the cheapest to restock: a single van trip serves four of them.

A trip here is one unlock to one dock, however long the ride. A rider who docks to change a bike and unlocks again counts twice, and trips under 60 seconds are dropped as faulty unlocks.

The zone mix has been stable for a year, which makes rebalancing easier to plan. The night route restocks the centre, and the day vans absorb the swings caused by weather and events in the suburbs.

# Service levels

## Availability and repairs

Table 2. Service levels against targets (sample data)

| Measure | Target | July | August | September |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Station availability (%) | 92 | 95 | 94 | 93 |
| Median rebalancing time (min) | 45 | 36 | 41 | 52 |
| Median repair turnaround (days) | 2.0 | 1.6 | 2.2 | 3.1 |
| Rider satisfaction (%) | 80 | 85 | 83 | 82 |

Source: station telemetry, workshop system and rider survey exports (sample)

Availability stayed above target in every month because the night route fills empty stations before demand arrives. Rebalancing time followed the repair backlog: with 60 e-bikes waiting in the workshop in September, vans had fewer working bikes to move and spent longer searching for stock.

## Satisfaction and complaints

Satisfaction held above target in every month, although it slipped by three points between July and September. Complaints about unavailable bikes fell from 318 to 244, and the share that mentioned a flat battery rose from 21 % to 35 %. The complaints that remained were mostly about e-bikes that showed a full charge on the app and then stopped, a fault that sits with the battery supplier but is within reach of the workshop to correct.

# Workshop and cost

## Mechanics and workload

Two experienced mechanics left in late August for roles at the city bus depot. Replacing a mechanic takes about twelve weeks from advert to working alone: five weeks to hire, four weeks of training and three weeks paired with a senior colleague.

Table 3. Repair jobs and staffing by month (sample data)

| Measure | July | August | September |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Repair jobs opened | 1,420 | 1,560 | 1,610 |
| Mechanics on the rota | 9 | 9 | 7 |
| Jobs per mechanic per working day | 7.2 | 7.9 | 10.5 |

Note: 22 working days a month. Source: rota of the last working day of each month; workshop system (sample)

A mechanic advertised in October would reach the bench in mid-January, which is why the request asks for the adverts now. The lead time of twelve weeks is the median of the last six hires.

## Cost per trip

Cost per trip fell from 2.35 to 2.20 because trips grew faster than the operating budget. The figure flatters the quarter slightly: it treats a member trip like a day-pass trip, although a day-pass rider is far more likely to leave a bike unlocked in the wrong place and add a van visit.

# Battery swap cabinets

Two measures are ready to start: lockable cabinets at stations where a rider or a van driver can swap a flat battery in about a minute, and a photo triage step in the app that lets the workshop close minor faults, such as a loose bell or a squeaking brake, without a visit. Together they target the two kinds of job that most often take a mechanic out of the workshop and onto the street. The estimate assumes that 40 % of battery jobs and 15 % of other minor jobs never reach the bench, which is what the cabinets achieved in the 6-week trial at three stations in August.

Table 4. Jobs the new measures are expected to avoid per quarter (sample data)

| Measure | Share of jobs (%) | Jobs per quarter | Resolved without a visit (%) | Jobs avoided |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Battery swap cabinets | 31 | 1,423 | 40 | 569 |
| Photo fault triage | 22 | 1,010 | 15 | 151 |
| Total | 53 | 2,433 | 30 | 720 |

Source: Q3 2026 repair jobs; August trial at three stations (sample)

Both measures reduce workshop load, so they matter most in a quarter like this one. At September's rate of 10.5 jobs per mechanic per working day, 720 avoided jobs a quarter is about one mechanic's work. That covers one of the two missing posts in a normal month. The spring peak, a third above normal volume, still needs both hires, which is why the cabinets and the posts are presented as one request.

# Outlook for the fourth quarter

Trips usually fall by a third between September and December as daylight shortens, so the backlog should clear without new staff before the end of the year. The spring season is the exposure. With two posts unfilled and no cabinets in place, the team expects repair turnaround above 4 days in March, twice the target, just as trips climb back past 140,000 a month. Advertising in October would bring the new mechanics to the bench in mid-January. That is before the season starts. Waiting until January would land them in April.

If both requests slip, the workshop would need overtime of about 400 hours before May to hold turnaround near target, at roughly one and a half times the hourly cost of regular time. The steering group meets once more before the winter works freeze.
