In today's fast-paced world, it's easy to forget the small rituals that shape our days. For me, that ritual was the 7:40 bus.

For six years I took the same bus to work. It wasn't just a commute; it was a classroom. I learned the driver's name in the first month (Teresa), and by the second year I knew which passengers would get off at the hospital and which would ride to the end of the line.

Most mornings Teresa would wait an extra thirty seconds for the woman with the walking frame who lived above the bakery. Nobody complained. On Teresa's last day before retirement, someone taped a card to the fare box, and by the end of the route it had forty signatures.

When the route was cut in 2023, I started cycling. The ride is faster, about 22 minutes instead of 35. But I miss the people. Ultimately, this experience stands as a testament to the profound power of human connection.
