We measured air temperature 1.5 m above the ground on 60 residential streets in one city during July and August 2025, with fixed sensors that logged every 10 minutes. At 15:00, streets with more than 40% tree canopy cover were on average 1.8 °C cooler than streets with less than 10% cover; at 03:00 the difference was 0.4 °C. The models included wind speed and building height as covariates. The cooling was strongest on east-west streets, where trees shaded the road for most of the afternoon, and weakest on north-south streets with buildings taller than 20 m. Street trees may reduce daytime heat exposure. One city and one summer limit how far the result carries to other places.
